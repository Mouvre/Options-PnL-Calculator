Market time runs across from the current time — now — to 1:00pm PT expiry (not from the 6:30am open) and SPY price runs up the side. The main line is the breakeven price: the SPY price at which your trade is worth exactly what you paid, if you sold at that moment. The drifting line is theta

Early in the day the breakeven line hugs your strike, because the contract still holds time value you can sell back. As expiry nears, that time value is gone, so SPY has to travel the full premium past the strike just for you to break even — the line bends away, fastest in the last hour.

Which side of the line are you on?

For a call, any SPY price above the breakeven line at that time means the contract is worth more than you paid; below it, less. Puts flip: profit is underneath the line. The further SPY sits from the line, the bigger the gain or loss — hover the chart for the exact dollars at any moment, or check the stats and the grid.

Breakeven needs a real move

Buying at the money means SPY has to move past the strike plus the premium you paid before expiry — for a call that's strike + premium, for a put strike − premium. The later in the day it is, the bigger the move you need per hour left.

Priced with Black-Scholes at the IV above, held constant across the chart. Real fills differ: the bid/ask spread, IV rising or crushing after a catalyst, and a mid-price limit that only fills if someone meets you all move your actual PnL off these lines. Expiry is 1:00pm PT the same day.
